## Decisions
- **Plain WebGL1, no library.** One quad and one fragment shader; Three.js was rejected for bundle size. The shader source is built in TypeScript (`shader.ts`) from exported palette and tuning constants so tests can assert the contrast floor and the reveal coverage without a GPU.
- **Time-continuous motion.** Every animated term is a sine of `u_time`; `t` accumulates frame deltas (clamped) and is never reset, so there is no loop seam. Pausing simply stops accumulating.
- **Heightfield cloth.** Vertical hanging folds (3 frequencies, slow sway of at most 3% of the viewport width) plus 3 travelling wave octaves (phase speed at most 0.15 viewport widths per second). Normals come from finite differences of the heightfield; diffuse shading plus a low specular sheen.
- **Reveal.** `reveal.ts` is pure: `advance(dtSeconds)` walks a wait/active state machine using an injectable RNG, so paused time is excluded by construction (the renderer does not call it while hidden). The envelope is a smootherstep rise, hold and fall (zero slope at both ends). The shader moves a curved edge across the view toward one side (`u_side`) and fades the cloth out behind it.
- **Fallback.** `ExploreVeil` renders the canvas first; if the renderer cannot start or the context is lost it switches to `.explore-veil-fallback`. Reduced motion (re-evaluated on change) and the low-power heuristic select the fallback without ever creating a context.
- **Performance.** DPR capped at 1.5, backing store scaled by 0.6, resize flagged by a window listener and applied in the next frame.
